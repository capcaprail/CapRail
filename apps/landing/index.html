The legend on your share certificate, enforced by the token.

CapRail keeps a private company’s cap table on Solana. Who may receive its shares is checked by the token itself — a Token-2022 mint with a transfer hook — on every transfer, whichever app sends it. A transfer that fails the check does not happen.

Restrictive legend

  1. 1. These shares may be transferred only to a holder admitted by the company’s compliance officer. Enforced. The hook refuses NotAccredited. Since v0.1.0.
  2. 2. An admission that has expired or been revoked admits nothing. Enforced. AccreditationExpired; the next transfer after a revocation is refused.
  3. 3. Shares not yet vested may not be transferred. Not yet. Vesting arrives with v0.3.0.
  4. 4. The company has a right of first refusal on any sale. Not yet. ROFR arrives with v0.4.0; until then the policy refuses to switch it on.

On paper, the legend is a promise. In CapRail, it is a check.

Before

The shareholders’ agreement says “admitted investors only”. The certificate prints it. A wallet can still send the token to anyone, and the company finds out afterwards — if it finds out at all.

After

The policy is data the token reads on every transfer. A transfer to a wallet the company has not admitted is refused by the network; balances on both sides stay as they were, and the refusal is in the company’s journal with its reason.

What it does

Admission is a property of the token

A compliance officer admits a wallet, with an expiry. The token’s transfer hook reads that record on every transfer and refuses anything else. A changed policy or status applies to the next transfer — no reissue, nothing for holders to do.

The cap table is read from the chain

Holders, amounts and shares of the issue update from the transactions themselves; every attempt, settled or refused, is in the company’s journal with its reason, time and parties.

A market that checks the buyer first

A holder offers shares at a fixed price; only wallets that would pass admission see the offer. Shares and payment move in one transaction, or neither does, and the platform fee is shown before accepting.

What CapRail does not do

  • It does not verify anyone’s accreditation. The compliance officer sets admission inside the product; there is no KYC or KYB provider, and the demo does not prove that a status matches a real person.
  • It does not issue or sell shares, or wrap them legally. No offering, no SPV, no shareholders’ agreement, no trading-venue licence. It enforces rules the company has already agreed.
  • It does not enforce vesting or a right of first refusal yet (v0.3.0, v0.4.0), and it does not produce the period report yet (v0.5.0). Jurisdiction and investor type are recorded, not enforced.
  • It does not hold your shares. An offer is a delegation, not escrow: a seller who moves the shares away leaves the offer standing, and the market marks it stale with the reason.
  • It runs on Solana devnet only. The payment token is a demo stablecoin, not USDC.
  • Not every wallet can send these tokens yet. A wallet that does not add the transfer hook’s accounts fails before the check runs — nothing moves, but nothing is sent either.

Questions

What stops someone from sending the token outside your app?
Nothing in the app — the check is in the token. Token-2022 calls the transfer hook on every transfer of this mint, from any program or wallet.
Who decides who is admitted?
The company’s compliance officer, with their own key. The administrator cannot change admission statuses.
Can I bring a token I already issued?
No. The policy is attached when the token is created; tokens issued elsewhere are out of scope.
Is this on mainnet?
No. Everything runs on Solana devnet; going to mainnet is a separate decision.
